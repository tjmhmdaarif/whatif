## Context

A partner university offers a one-semester exchange. It is a good language for my
minor and a city I want to spend a season in. Costs are higher than a normal semester
and the application deadline is six weeks away.

## Options

**Exchange semester abroad**
- Around 4,000 dollars of fees and living costs on top of my normal budget
- Courses in the local language
- 15 credits that may or may not count toward my program
- New city, new routines

**Stay and take extra electives**
- Normal cost, no extra travel
- Credits guaranteed to count
- Two more electives at my own university
- Another term in the same city

## Decision

Chose the exchange. Language and personal growth carried the decision, and I believed
the credits would transfer back to my program without extra paperwork.

## Outcome

Reviewed 2026-09-10. Result: worse.

The credits did not transfer back to my program without extra paperwork. Two of the
four courses I took map to nothing on my home transcript and I am appealing one of
them, which is unresolved. I have to make up the credit hours next term, which means a
heavier load than the exchange was supposed to replace.

Living costs were also not the same as a semester at home. Rent in the host city was
about double what I budgeted and the fee structure was harder to predict. I had to
draw from savings, and I returned with a much smaller margin than I planned.

The parts I expected to work did work. My language improved more in four months than
in two years of classes, and I would make the same choice again for that reason alone.
But I treated the exchange as a straight swap for a normal semester and it was not,
and both of the assumptions that broke were about logistics I had not checked.
